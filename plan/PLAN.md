# Chamber talk + deep dive: the plan

Michael Harrison and Chris Carpio, presenting at Business Connections (Morris County Chamber), February 2027.
Then a hands-on deep dive at the Chamber the following week.

**The goal in one line:** turn a room of business owners into people who show up the next week, and leave that session with a framework for their own business that reads like a proposal.

---

## 1. The talk (February, Business Connections)

Length still to confirm with the Chamber. This fits 15–20 minutes.

| Time | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Hook: "How many apps did you open before 9 AM?" Hands up. | Michael |
| 1:00 | The framework video (60 s, `render/`) | — |
| 2:00 | The idea: same core for every business, your industry on top, four doors, then what's only yours | Chris |
| 5:00 | Live click-through: our click-through demo, picking a business type someone in the room shouts out | Chris drives, Michael talks |
| 9:00 | The custom add-on story: Michael's networking calendar. Show it for real: events at the places he goes, one click to sign up, it lands on his calendar | Michael |
| 12:00 | Proof: what we've already built (coffee ordering + rewards, construction time clock, tattoo studio, video production portal). Only name the ones whose owners said yes | Both |
| 14:00 | The ask: next week's deep dive + the QR code | Michael |
| 15:00 | Q&A | Both |

**Say it carefully:** the networking calendar's "one click" is true for most event sites. Chamber forms still end with an "I'm not a robot" box, which a person ticks. Say "one click from my calendar" rather than "no clicks at all," or show an event that doesn't have that box.

## 2. The QR code (last slide, and on a table card)

Scanning opens one short page (works on a phone, under a minute):

1. Name, business, email, phone (optional)
2. **What kind of business?** (pick list from our demo's business types, plus "Other")
3. **"What's the one thing you're juggling that you wish ran itself?"** (one line; this is what makes their framework personal)
4. **"I'll be at the deep dive on [date]"**: yes / can't make it, send me mine

The slide says, in plain words:

> **Next week, we build one live.**
> Come to the deep dive at the Chamber. We'll pick one business in the room and build its framework on the spot.
> Everyone who comes leaves with their own framework, made before you walk in.

Keep it free to enter with nothing to buy, so it's a giveaway, not a raffle. We **choose** the build (the business that shows the idea best), rather than drawing a name from a hat.

Where the page lives is open (see section 7). Sign-ups should land straight in the portal pipeline, not a spreadsheet.

## 3. Between the talk and the deep dive (that week)

- **Same night:** thank-you email to everyone who scanned. Link to the video, the date and room, and "reply with anything else you want us to know about your business."
- **Days 1–5:** build each RSVP's framework ahead of time from their answers: core, their industry pieces, which of the four doors matter most to them, and one custom add-on idea from their "one thing" line. This is mostly automated from our demo's data; a person reads each before it goes out.
- **Pick the live build** by day 4 (one business, a good story, owner happy to be on screen). Tell them privately so they're sure to come; everyone else still gets theirs.
- **Day before:** reminder email with the room, parking, and "your framework is ready, it's waiting for you there."
- People who said "can't make it, send me mine": send it the morning after the deep dive, with an offer to walk them through it on a 20-minute call.

## 4. The deep dive (the next week, at the Chamber)

About 75–90 minutes. Chamber room, screen, Wi-Fi, coffee.

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Welcome, the video again (it's short) |
| 0:05 | How the framework works, slower than the talk. Owner / team / client / website views |
| 0:20 | **The live build.** The chosen business: we ask the questions, fill in their framework on screen, and click through what their portal would look like |
| 0:50 | Everyone gets theirs: printed one-pager + a link. Quick table time: Chris and Michael walk the room |
| 1:10 | What a build looks like: the steps, the timeline, how pricing works (fixed price, quoted after a sketch) |
| 1:20 | One next step: book a 20-minute call before you leave (QR or a sign-up sheet) |

**What each person takes home (the "basic framework" = their proposal):**
1. Their business on one page: the core, their industry layer, the four doors, one custom add-on idea
2. What it would replace: the apps they named, and what those cost today
3. The steps: free look → paid sketch (credited to the build) → fixed-price build → monthly care
4. A link to book a call

## 5. Follow-up after the deep dive

| When | Who gets it | What |
|---|---|---|
| Same day | Everyone who came | Thank-you + their framework link + book-a-call link |
| Day 1 | RSVPs who didn't come | Their framework + "want us to walk you through it?" |
| Day 3 | Anyone who hasn't booked | Short note with one idea specific to their business |
| Day 7 | The live-build business | A short video of their portal mock-up, ask to share it |
| Day 10 | Everyone | The live-build video (with permission), "here's what one looked like" |
| Day 14 | Anyone still open | Last personal note from whoever they talked to |
| Day 30 | Everyone not yet a client | Check-in, invite to the next Chamber thing we're at |

Every touch is logged against the person in the pipeline, so neither of us double-sends. Every email is read by a person before it goes.

## 7. Decisions still open

1. The talk's exact date and length, and the deep dive's date and room (ask the Chamber now; February books early).
2. Our name. What do we call the company on the slide and the QR page?
3. Where the sign-up page lives (our own site vs. a page on the portal).
4. Do prices go on the deep-dive handout, or only "fixed price after a sketch"?
5. Which past builds we can name on stage (owners need to say yes).
6. How we choose the live-build business, and what "built" means for them afterward (mock-up only, or a real starter build).
7. Who runs the slides: a short deck around the video, or video + live demo only.
